         The data package contains the results from a multi-institutional winter limnology sampling campaign on the Laurentian Great Lakes. Researchers from 19 institutions sampled 49 locations in all five of the Great Lakes over a period of 24 days in February-March 2022. This dataset contains information on diverse physical, chemical, and biological parameters.

               The Winter Grab study involved sampling at 49 locations across the Great Lakes. Samples were collected between Feb. 02, 2022 and March 10, 2022, with most sampling taking place in mid-February. Samples were collected from the ice, piers, and US and Canadian Coast Guard vessels. Sites ranged considerably in depth (1-93 m). Thirty two sites were ice-covered and 17 sites were ice-free. Sampling crews documented site conditions, including snow and ice cover, and measured light penetration at 22 locations. Water profiles were collected with CTD sondes, and water and plankton samples were taken from various depths for lab analysis. Samples were analyzed for water chemistry, pigments, cyanotoxins, and planktonic abundance and community composition. Microbial and primary production rate measurements were performed on some of the samples.
                
               Phosphorus and nitrogen levels in water samples were analyzed using EPA methods, while alkalinity and chloride were measured via titration and ion selective electrode methods, respectively. Organic matter was characterized using spectrofluorometry, and chlorophyll, phycocyanin, and phycoerythrin concentrations were measured using fluorometry and spectrophotometry. We also measured microcystins and other cyanobacterial metabolites, using ELISA and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry. Bacterial abundances were determined using flow cytometry. Phytoplankton, microzooplankton, and crustacean zooplankton abundance and community composition were assessed using microscopy. Bacterial and primary production rates were determined through radiolabeled isotope incorporation and photosynthesis-irradiance experiments.
                
               Technical validation and quality assurance/control procedures were performed for all published data. Data outliers, accounting for less than 0.5% of all data, were removed based on visual inspection and expert judgment when errors were evident. Metadata consistency was maintained across all files, ensuring uniform use of Principal Investigators' names and sampling station names. The order of data columns was also standardized, following a consistent format: Principal Investigator name, sampling station, sample depth, sample names, parameter values, units, and notes.

               Pmax (g C/g Chl a/hr) is a measure of the maximum photosynthetic potential or productivity rate that can be achieved per unit of chlorophyll a, typically under optimal conditions. This parameter is crucial for comparing the productivity efficiency of different phytoplankton communities or under varying environmental conditions. Alpha (α) is the initial slope of the Photosynthesis-Irradiance (P-I) curve at low light intensities. It represents the photosynthetic efficiency of phytoplankton under low light conditions. A higher α value indicates that phytoplankton are more efficient at utilizing low levels of light for photosynthesis. It reflects the ability of the photosynthetic apparatus to convert light into chemical energy (carbon fixation) when light is not limiting. The unit [g C/g Chl a/hr]/[µmol quanta/m²/s] is often used to express α, as it normalizes the rate of carbon fixation to both chlorophyll content and light intensity. Alpha (α) represents the efficiency of photosynthesis at low light levels, reflecting the organism's ability to capitalize on available light. Beta (β) describes the rate of photoinhibition, or the decrease in photosynthetic rate, at very high light intensities. It represents the decline in photosynthetic activity when light becomes excessive and potentially damaging. A higher β value indicates greater sensitivity to photoinhibition, meaning that the phytoplankton’s photosynthesis decreases more sharply under high light intensity. Conversely, a lower β suggests that the phytoplankton are more tolerant to high light conditions. Beta (β) captures the decrease in photosynthesis under excessive light, indicating how well the organism copes with high light stress.
